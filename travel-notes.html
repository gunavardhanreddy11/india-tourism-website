Before you go

When to travel, how to get around, what to wear, and how to travel lightly. Short and practical.

Seasons in one minute

October to March is pleasant across most of the country. April to June gets very hot on the plains, so head for the hills. June to September is monsoon: Ladakh and Spiti are at their best, the Western Ghats turn green, and beaches go quiet.

Getting around

Book trains on IRCTC. Popular routes sell out weeks ahead; the Tatkal quota opens the day before travel (10 am for AC classes, 11 am for non-AC). Flights between big cities are frequent and often cheap. In cities, use the Metro where there is one, app cabs, or prepaid taxi counters at airports and stations. In autos, agree on the fare first or ask for the meter.

Dress and etiquette

Take your shoes off at temples, gurdwaras, mosques and most homes. Cover shoulders and knees at religious sites, and your head at gurdwaras. Use your right hand to eat and to hand things over. Ask before photographing people, and expect some temples to restrict phones, leather or entry.

Money, phones, health

UPI QR codes are everywhere, even at tea stalls, but carry some cash for small towns and hill areas. Drink bottled or filtered water and ease into street food. At altitude (Ladakh, Spiti), acclimatise before going higher. The national emergency number is 112.

Travel lightly

Carry a refillable bottle and bring your plastic back down from treks. Hire local guides and buy crafts from the people who make them. Don't feed or crowd wildlife on safaris. Parts of the Northeast (Arunachal, Nagaland, Mizoram, Manipur) need an Inner Line Permit for Indian visitors, and some areas need a Protected Area Permit for foreigners. Check the state tourism site first.
